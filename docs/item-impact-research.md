# Toward a trustworthy item guide

The current `pipeline/wpa.py` predicts the outcome of a match from the state before a purchase, then reports whether buyers won more often than predicted. The 10-minute curve shows how the *game* evolved after buying. A high AUC or a reproducible curve says the predictions or associations are stable; neither proves the item caused the subsequent win. The 2026-09-24 export was trained on 8,502 games and reports AUC 0.773 and calibration error 0.25 percentage points; those are outcome-prediction metrics, not item-effect validation. The corrected cross-fitting code was used for that run, but no item-impact slot passed the split-half reliability gate.

## Data we already have

Riot Match-V5 details and timelines provide final outcome, champion/role/opponent/draft, timestamped item purchases and undos, and periodic player frames. The pipeline already keeps patch-specific item recipes and prices from Data Dragon, reconstructs complete item order, and takes only frames strictly before each purchase. See `pipeline/engine.py` and `pipeline/wpa.py`. Riot documents [Match-V5](https://developer.riotgames.com/apis/) and [Data Dragon item fields](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/lol). The collector should continue respecting Riot's [application/method/service limits and `Retry-After`](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/portal); more API calls alone will not fix confounding.

What is *not* observed precisely: gold and inventory at the exact purchase instant (frames are periodic), the set of items truly affordable then, the player's intent, historical rank at that game, and all skill and team-coordination differences. The current `pre_features()` explicitly marks exact budget unavailable. Filling these gaps matters more than using a larger classifier.

## Next estimator, in order

1. Define one decision: among players on the same champion, role, patch, item slot and comparable pre-purchase state, compare finishing item A with item B. Only compare alternatives with overlap in observed game state and plausible recipe/budget. Keep matchup-specific results separate; use all-matchup data as a prior for sparse matchups, not as if every opponent were the same.
2. Fit two nuisance models on earlier matches only: probability of choosing A and probability of winning conditional on the choice and *pre-purchase* covariates. Combine them with a doubly robust score. `pipeline/item_research.py` now runs this research-only pairwise comparison using the existing forward-held-out evaluator in `pipeline/model.py`. It reports overlap, effective sample size, cluster-aware intervals and predictive quality, and keeps `causalValidationPassed` false. [EconML's DR documentation](https://www.pywhy.org/EconML/spec/estimation/dr.html) explains the method and its observed-confounder assumption.
3. Add better pre-decision controls: lane/opponent gold and XP, full inventory/recipe, prior purchase history, recent combat/objectives, patch, side, draft, player history *before* this match, and item availability. Check missingness by item and matchup. Never feed post-purchase state or final inventory into the treatment/outcome models. The prior outcome-derived draft-strength features are temporarily excluded from the WPA retraining code: their cross-fold encoding could indirectly carry scored-match outcomes into other folds. Reintroduce draft adjustment only with fold-local encoding or exogenous features.
4. Validate on later patches and players, with propensity overlap, effective sample size, calibration, placebo outcomes, sensitivity to unobserved confounding, and stability across regions. [EconML's validation guide](https://www.pywhy.org/EconML/spec/validation.html) notes that the counterfactual is unobservable and sensitivity analysis remains necessary.
5. Only after single decisions survive these checks, estimate a *sequence* of item decisions and evaluate the complete build policy on held-out matches. A three-item core is not the sum of three independent item effects: first item changes later gold, timing and available choices. [Dynamic DML](https://www.pywhy.org/EconML/spec/estimation_dynamic.html) addresses adaptive treatments over time, but still requires the relevant time-varying confounders to be recorded. Policy evaluation should compare a proposed route with a baseline under overlap, then display conservative uncertainty and abstain on sparse matchups.

The website currently shows the most played complete three-item route and alternative observed routes, with a themed path animation that gently changes bend every 500 ms. Its win rate and adjusted model readouts are explicitly observational. Do not promote an item or build as causally "best" until the held-out policy comparison and confounder checks above are credible.

Run a read-only local comparison with `\.venv\Scripts\python.exe pipeline\item_research.py --champion Sett --role TOP --slot 1 --item-a 6631 --item-b 3153`; add `--opponent Garen` for a specific matchup. On the collection at 2026-09-24 03:43 UTC, the all-opponent Sett example had 281 eligible decisions, below the evaluator's 400-game minimum, so it correctly returned `insufficient_data` without an effect estimate. The exact count will change as the collector runs.
